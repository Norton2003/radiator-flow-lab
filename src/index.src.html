How it's calculated

Air side

Heat transfer uses the Chang & Wang (1997) louver-fin Colburn factor, fitted to a broad database of louvered-fin test cores:

j = ReLp−0.49(θ/90)0.27(Fp/Lp)−0.14(Fl/Lp)−0.29(Td/Lp)−0.23(Ll/Lp)0.68(Tp/Lp)−0.28(δf/Lp)−0.05

Friction uses the Kim & Bullard (2002) flat-tube louver correlation, with entrance and exit losses added. Fin efficiency is the straight-fin tanh(mL)/mL with L = half the fin height.

h = j·G·cp·Pr−2/3   ΔP = (G²/2ρ)[Kc + 1 − σ² + f·A/Ac − (1 − σ² − Ke)]

Airflow you actually get

In Drive mode the face velocity is solved, not assumed: the core's pressure drop must equal the ram pressure your duct recovers (Cp·½ρv²) plus the fan's pressure at that flow (a straight-line fan curve from shutoff pressure to free-air flow). Air density follows site altitude.

Coolant side and overall

Flat single-port tubes, laminar Nu = 7.54 with a developing-flow boost, Gnielinski above Re 4000, blended in between. Overall conductance and effectiveness:

1/UA = 1/(ηohaAa) + 1/(hcAc) + Rwall
ε = 1 − exp[(1/Cr)·NTU0.22(e−CrNTU0.78 − 1)]   (crossflow, both unmixed)

The face map marches each tube in 48 cells so the coolant cools along its length and each fin row heats its own air.

What to trust

  • Correlations typically land within ±15–25% of test data inside their range. Flags above show when you leave it.
  • The louver slice is 2D and periodic: great for flow regime and angle, not for absolute ΔP.
  • Fin pattern assumes 85% louver length, single row of flat tubes, side tanks, single pass.
  • Check FSAE rules on coolant: glycol is shown for comparison only.
  • Use it to choose a direction, then confirm with CFD (ANSYS or SimScale) and dyno or MoTeC data.

Sources: Chang, Y.J. & Wang, C.C., Int. J. Heat Mass Transfer 40(3), 1997, doi. Kim, M.H. & Bullard, C.W., Int. J. Refrigeration 25, 2002. Gnielinski, 1976. Incropera, Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer (ε-NTU).
